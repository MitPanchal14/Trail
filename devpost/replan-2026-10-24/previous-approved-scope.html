First user

You, doing difficult research.

You can plan and understand explanations, but applying them and returning after a break are harder. Long gaps erase the context you need.

Helping similar people is a future possibility. Their needs and demand are not yet validated.

Core idea

A trail worth returning to.
A place saved for you.

See the effort already recorded. Find where you stopped and one concrete action to continue. Pausing should feel ordinary, not shameful.

This is a product hypothesis. We still need to test whether it helps you resume.

One small loop, repeated.

Plan → review steps → start one → complete it or save your place → revisit progress and resume. The app uses what you record; it does not observe your external work.

Experience priorities

Next action first.

The current action leads the dashboard. The trail stays prominent. The full day's plan sits below or in a compact timeline.

Check-ins take 20–30 seconds at most. Notes are optional. Progress is private by default. Avoid excessive pop-ups.

Honest progress

Pause without pretending.

Completed actions, time spent, and pauses must remain distinguishable. A checkbox is your report; time is not proof of productivity.

Exact trail growth, time controls, visual style, and blocked-task handling are decisions for the PRD.

Proposed visual direction · illustrative entries

A journey you can read, not just a line that grows.

Completed actions become named checkpoints you can revisit. Your current action has an active marker. Saving your place leaves a resting marker with context, so the trail shows where to continue without treating a pause as failure.

Completed checkpoints record what changed. A resting marker preserves unfinished work. The dotted stretch is a possible next action, not earned progress.

Useful

Select a checkpoint to revisit the completed action and its recorded context. Select your saved place to find where you stopped. Time can accompany a record; it does not imply completion.

Positive

A warm landscape, a small completion animation, and a journey that remains intact through quiet days. No punitive resets or broken-streak warnings.

Empty: a welcoming starting point and first action. One completion: the journey visibly begins. Several sessions: readable accumulated progress. Illustration style, layout, animation, and growth rules are PRD decisions; this sketch proposes the meaning of the trail.

The approved boundary

Open each section to inspect what belongs in the first experiment. Precise UI and technical decisions follow in PRD and spec.

Now Prove the work-and-return loop
  • Today's tasks and brief time/busyness context.
  • Editable AI-suggested small steps and a simple day plan.
  • One current action, lightweight session/time recording, completion, and Save my place.
  • Saved context, a revisitable trail, and persistence across visits and days.
  • A deployed web app, a short end-to-end demo, and public source with planning docs.
Later Useful after the core earns its place
  • Recurring routines and richer scheduling: more planning complexity.
  • A desktop/floating widget: platform work beyond the first web deployment.
  • Optional sharing cards: private progress is enough to test the core.
  • Testing and tailoring for other people: demand remains unvalidated.
Cut Different problems or unnecessary distraction
  • Research tutoring, automated literature reviews, and novelty assessment: beyond this product's job.
  • Automatic observation of other apps or verified-productivity claims: unsupported by self-reports.
  • Social feeds, competitive leaderboards, and aggressive reminders: unnecessary for this personal loop.
The proof

Leave. Return. Know where to begin.

Enter a real research task → review suggested steps → start one → save a brief context note → reload → find the same place and an action to resume → complete an action → revisit its trail entry.

Test with real work: does a check-in fit within 30 seconds, and do you actually resume after a pause? A working demo proves the app's behavior; it does not prove improved consistency.

Still to decide

In the PRD: commitment controls, trail rules, time recording, blocked-work next steps, and what happens if you leave without saving. In the technical spec: AI provider, storage, and hosting. The name is provisional.

My critique: contextual return is the strongest part. The trail gives it a visual identity, but generic AI task breakdown is not enough differentiation. The experience must make resuming genuinely easier without adding another planning burden.
