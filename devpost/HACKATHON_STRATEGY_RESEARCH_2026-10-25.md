# Trail: critical product and hackathon strategy

25 October 2026. Research decision memo, following DEEP_RESEARCH_PLAN_2026-10-25.md. This expands the earlier critical review and cloud demo research; it does not replace their technical receipts or authorize another feature batch.

## Verdict

Trail has a coherent, unusually careful local prototype. It does not yet have evidence of a compelling reason to adopt another work tool. The main competitive risk is ordinary utility with attractive scenery, not insufficient animation. A timer, checklist, note and mountain are individually established ideas. The combined experience needs to make leaving and returning to unfinished work noticeably easier for a person, and make that value immediately visible to a judge.

The best remaining strategy is: finish the approved correction, prove a real interruption-and-return, remove the biggest discovery obstacle, then protect delivery. One small useful addition is possible if observation supports it. A broad feature expansion would consume the time needed to demonstrate the product we already have.

There is no defensible win probability. The event gallery is not published, so actual competing entries cannot be compared. This is targeted research across official rules, first-party products, original research and design guidance, not an exhaustive survey of the Internet.

## What is actually ready

The reconciled cloud version implements real goal/actions, conservative timing, pause context, a work reference, local persistence, explicit Resume, transactional Retry, limited Undo, final review, history and guarded reopening. The mountain now has one travelling expedition and a campsite tied to useful saved context. The earlier independent review passed the imported runtime's syntax, 22 Node tests, build and ten Chromium gates.

OpenCode's newer END_FOCUS_BLOCK_EVIDENCE.md reports a stopped block can end without completing the action, with 25 tests and eleven Chromium gates. **That correction still needs independent review.** Its receipt is not a fresh verification by this research pass. User visual acceptance, physical-device/background checks, human value, public access and recording remain different gates.

The product still has important boundaries: one open goal; manual capture; browser-and-origin storage; no sync/import, AI, background capture or closed-site reminders. Blank notes preserve older instructions; explicit clearing of old instruction/context remains a separate known correction. None of these facts should be hidden by a polished recording.

## 1. Optimize for the actual competition

The [official rules](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/rules) give equal weight to Design, Potential Impact, Innovation/Idea and Presentation. They also require a working end-to-end proof of concept, public source with planning documents and a video; under three minutes is the stated recommendation. Stage-one eligibility includes Skill Pack use. An AI feature inside the app is not listed as a requirement. Public source is required; an open-source license is recommended in the wording. Installed skill metadata alone does not establish actual use.

| Criterion | Current assessment — judgment, not a judge's score | Highest-value evidence/change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design | Coherent accepted composition; terrain is distinctive. Tiny symbols and first-use discovery may still obscure its meaning. | Someone recognizes saved place, review and finish without narration. Preserve layout; correct one observed obstacle. |
| Potential Impact | Plausible problem, but no observed benefit or evidence of repeat use. | A concrete work artifact and an observed interruption/return; disclose the small sample. |
| Innovation/Idea | Weakest area: familiar ingredients; combination is not automatically novel. | Explain the deliberate handoff → campsite → return → confirmed finish interaction and acknowledge alternatives. |
| Presentation | Strong drafts, but a draft is not a rehearsed functioning demonstration. | Public usable build, real resource, short recording of the full loop. |

The organizer's [written project-review recap](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/updates/47209-will-it-ship-recording-is-up) emphasizes understandable purpose, realistic content, distinctive design and quick access to the app. It supports clearer execution, not adding features for their own sake. I reviewed the recap, not the full recorded session. The [unpublished gallery](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/project-gallery) prevents claims that Trail is better than this event's field.

## 2. The competitive challenge is stronger than a to-do list

These are documented capabilities, not hands-on comparative benchmarks or claims of feature exclusivity.

| Alternative | First-party evidence | Consequence for Trail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workona | [Tasks and notes](https://workona.com/help/workona-tasks-notes/) attach work links/files and descriptions; its [tab manager](https://workona.com/help/tab-manager/) preserves/restores browser workspaces with its extension. | Context plus a link is not novel. Trail must justify its deliberate, lightweight return ritual without claiming automatic workspace restoration. |
| Sunsama | [Daily shutdown](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/changelog/daily-shutdown) supports reviewing, reflecting and packing up work. | The stopping ritual is established. Trail's specific unfinished-action handoff must be clearer and lighter. |
| Llama Life | [How it works](https://llamalife.co/howitworks) centers one-task timeboxing, adjustable durations and small starts. | Timers and calm focus presentation are insufficient differentiation. A 20-minute minimum may deter someone trying to restart; test that hypothesis. |
| Amazing Marvin | [Task/project notes](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/amazing-marvin/task-and-project-notes) and [sequential projects](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/amazing-marvin/working-with-projects) cover notes and ordered next actions. | Do not pitch task notes or sequencing as a new invention. |
| Session | [Product](https://www.stayinsession.com/) and [changelog](https://stayinsession.com/changelog) describe focus, intention and reflection workflows. | Reflection is not an exclusive advantage either. |
| Forest | [Product](https://www.forestapp.cc/) ties focus to nature-based growth. | A nature reward is a design vocabulary, not a unique product proposition. |
| Pieces | [Long-term memory documentation](https://docs.pieces.app/products/desktop/configuration/long-term-memory) describes automatically captured workflow context. | Automation is a serious longer-term alternative. Trail's current manual/local design is a tradeoff, not proof of superior privacy or recall. |
| A note beside the work | A user's existing document already accepts text and a next instruction. No migration is required. | This is the strongest adoption question: does Trail save enough reconstruction effort to repay setup and capture? |

The defensible current positioning is narrow in the problem, broad in the audience: **“For work you have to leave unfinished. Save the next move and the place to use it.”** A student, freelancer or builder can all have this problem. One concrete demo story does not require a student-only pivot.

Do not claim Trail is the first interruption tool, an automatic context restorer, or clinically helpful for ADHD. The possible distinction is the quality of a small interaction system, not an unoccupied category.

## 3. What the behavioural evidence supports

### A useful cue is stronger than a generic motivational sentence

[Leroy and Glomb's 2018 research](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/tasks-interrupted-how-anticipating-time-pressure-on-resumption-of/) reports four studies supporting ready-to-resume planning. Its central outcome concerns attention residue and performance on the **interrupting task**. It does not establish that Trail makes users resume faster or improves their productivity.

[Parnin and DeLine's programming research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/evaluating-cues-for-resuming-interrupted-programming-tasks/) combines a survey of 371 programmers with a controlled study of resumption cues. It makes concrete context cues a credible direction. Its automated programming cues and task setting differ from Trail's manual notes; its results must not become Trail marketing statistics.

**Design inference:** encourage a usable instruction with a location and unresolved constraint. “Finish slides” is weak. “In slide 4, replace the capacity estimate; confirm the room booking before sending” gives the returning person something to do. A prompt example can improve the cue without adding a required field or AI model.

### There is no verified dopamine colour recipe here

[Sailer and colleagues' randomized simulation study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Michael-Sailer-2/publication/311879391_How_gamification_motivates_An_experimental_study_of_the_effects_of_specific_game_design_elements_on_psychological_need_satisfaction/links/5874ebdc08ae329d62202795/How-gamification-motivates-An-experimental-study-of-the-effects-of-specific-game-design-elements-on-psychological-need-satisfaction.pdf) found different psychological effects for different bundles of game elements; gamification was not effective as one generic treatment. This was an online simulation, not Trail, and not a measurement of dopamine. It cannot prescribe a palette or prove retention from a moving climber.

[Amabile's HBS discussion of small wins](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-small-wins-unleash-creativity) emphasizes progress in meaningful work. **Inference:** show the actual action accomplished, not points for interacting with the interface. Saving a stopping place deserves clear acknowledgement, but should not earn fake ascent or count as completed work.

[Duolingo's animation account](https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation/) describes working on comprehension before celebration and refining motion through iteration. This is a company's design account, not causal proof for Trail. Borrow its sequence: recognizable state → meaningful transition → restrained celebration. Do not borrow its mascot, assets or assume its results transfer.

Keep the existing palette's roles consistent: neutral terrain, visible current position, distinct earned path and limited warm summit accent. Check contrast and meaning without colour. These are interface choices, not neurological claims. More particles will not solve an unclear campsite.

### A streak could undermine the return-friendly promise

[University of Colorado's account of streak research](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch) describes how broken streaks can discourage continuation, including when the break is externally caused. [Duolingo's own experiment](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/) supports lowering the qualification barrier in its particular product. Neither establishes that streaks will improve Trail.

For a product about interrupted work, “your progress is still here” is more coherent than “you lost your streak.” Calendar rules also introduce timezone, Undo and grace-period work. Defer streaks for this submission. A history of actual finished work already provides continuity without a new reward economy.

## 4. The real gaps, with bounded responses

| Gap | Consequence | Response and acceptance gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benefit takes setup to discover | Judges may see another task manager before the saved-place value. | If observation confirms friction, add a visible **Try a saved place** entry using the existing isolated fictional mode. A person reaches and explains the saved instruction without coaching. Real records remain untouched. |
| Capture is another administrative step | The intended audience may abandon the ritual during a real interruption. | Observe one actual stop. Can the person leave a useful cue comfortably? Improve one prompt or disclosure if they struggle; do not require more metadata. |
| Work link is a placeholder | Open work looks cosmetic and fails to demonstrate continuation. | Use a real, user-owned work reference for recording, preferably a useful section anchor. Show it opening. Do not imply that opening it resumes timing or verifies external work. |
| Old instruction cannot be explicitly cleared | An obsolete plan can survive blank Save and mislead the return. | Separate correction after end-block review: explicit removal intent, save/Retry, reload and Undo tests. Blank/Skip must continue preserving by default. No casual change to blank semantics. |
| One open goal restricts real interruptions | A person may leave one project to do another; Trail cannot manage both as active expeditions. | Disclose the current one-goal scope. Do not remove ownership/replanning guards to simulate multi-goal support. Longer-term design work is needed. |
| Terrain may communicate decoration more than state | The intended signature feature does not teach the workflow. | Ask what campsite, all-checked review and summit mean. Correct scale/labels only if misunderstood. No new art pipeline today. |
| Local storage is easy to overpromise | Another device, port or deployment appears to lose work. | Explain browser/site boundary in product help and recording. Test the final origin fresh. Raw export without import is not a usable backup workflow. |
| Effort is not attention or progress | A timer and equal action slots can imply measurements they do not provide. | Keep recorded effort, checked actions and illustrative route separate. Avoid productivity percentages or “fastest finish” rewards. |

The first-use recommendation is an untested usability hypothesis. [NN/G's empty-state guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/) supports direct paths to key tasks; it does not prove this button is needed in Trail. Existing header controls may suffice if people find them. [Progressive disclosure guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) supports keeping secondary fields out of the first decision, while the core saved next move stays visible.

## 5. Creative candidates, including the ones to reject today

Effort ranges below are rough planning allowances including focused verification, not delivery promises. Do at most one optional addition after the correction, observation and delivery essentials are secure.

| Candidate | Product rationale | Cost/risk | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Try a saved place | Lets a stranger experience the defining moment before creating a goal. | Roughly 20–35 minutes if existing isolated fixture can be reused; must test no writes to real data, no timing start and mode exit. | First candidate if discovery is observed to fail. |
| Copy saved place | Portable plain-text brief: goal/action, next move, stopping context and work URL. Can be pasted beside the work. Addresses extra-app friction without integration infrastructure. | Roughly 25–40 minutes; clipboard rejection needs a visible selectable-text fallback. No mutation of canonical data/timing. Not sync, backup or restored external context. | Strongest new utility candidate; only if discovery already works and a person wants portability. |
| Five-minute start | A smaller first commitment may make restarting less daunting. | Roughly 20–35 minutes; duration validation, labels and test coverage must agree. No auto-completion or fake time. | Conditional on users resisting the current duration choices; not an evidence-free “dopamine” tweak. |
| Concrete finish receipt | Show the actual work completed and its real recorded effort, using existing history. Could make the summit meaningful beyond a quote. | Existing history may already suffice. Prefer recording that view before building a new card/export. | Reuse before adding. |
| Pause from the actual work tab | Solves capture friction closer to where it happens. A browser extension could capture an opted-in URL and a deliberate note. | Installation, permissions, distribution and privacy design. Much larger than one UI correction. | Strong longer-term direction; wrong final-hours bet. |
| AI next-move suggestion | Could help turn a vague note into an actionable one. | Actual document context, safe proxy/secrets, quality evaluation, acceptance/editing and manual fallback. A title-only suggestion adds little. | Defer. Rules do not require an in-app AI feature. |
| Multi-goal, sync or reminders | Useful real-product capabilities. | Changes persistence, ownership, scheduling or recovery; substantial new failure surface. | Defer beyond this submission's core loop. |
| New 3D art/Lottie/cinematic intro | Can produce initial visual appeal. | Asset rights, integration, performance, responsive/motion testing; no automatic utility gain. | Preserve accepted terrain. Only fix a demonstrated readability issue. |
| Sound, streak, points or leaderboard | Familiar engagement devices. | Browser/device/calendar rules and potential distraction or task-splitting incentives. | Defer. No evidence these are the present bottleneck. |

Suggested long-term experiment, not today's scope: let Trail act as a lightweight handoff layer for existing work rather than require replacing the user's task system. Compare deliberate manual capture, portable notes and an opted-in browser shortcut. Measure which actually gets used through interruptions before investing in an intelligent coach.

## 6. Obtain useful human evidence in 10–15 minutes

Ask two or three available people separately; do not delay delivery waiting for recruitment. If only the creator is available, label it a self-test. Tiny observations diagnose obstacles; they do not estimate market demand or prove efficacy.

1. Give a fresh browser and a concrete situation: “You were preparing a workshop update and had to leave. Show how you would find what to do next.” Do not point to buttons.
2. Record what they click, whether they can name the next action and find the reference, and every prompt they need. Ask what they think the campsite means.
3. Let them create one short action and save a cue for a real resource. Interrupt with a small unrelated task, then return and reload. Ask them to continue; watch rather than narrate.
4. Ask what they would otherwise use and whether Trail adds enough value to justify maintaining it. “I like the design” does not answer this question.
5. Ask whether all checked means finished and what Resume does. Show the final review if they miss the distinction.

Record individual observations, not fabricated percentages: person/task, cue quality, success without help, help needed, mistaken expectations and a verbatim comment with consent. Timing a few trials may reveal friction, but do not advertise an improvement rate or call it an A/B experiment. Any comparison with a document note needs comparable tasks and balanced order before even a cautious interpretation.

Ship a correction if it removes a repeated or severe obstacle. Do not redesign around a passing aesthetic preference. If people cannot explain a reason to use Trail over a document note, acknowledge that the value proposition remains unvalidated rather than adding more effects.

## 7. Preserve trust and verify the actual demo environment

| Check | Why it matters now | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent end-block review | Must permit replanning without falsely completing work; verify abort/Retry and exact preserved context/effort. | OpenCode reports passing; this research did not rerun it. |
| Physical Mac sleep/return and final-origin reload | Lifecycle suspension/discard is real; local automated fixtures are incomplete coverage. | [Chrome lifecycle guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/page-lifecycle-api) explains suspension/discard. Do not imply physical testing occurred. |
| Primary browser on intended phone/desktop | A 390px screenshot is not a physical phone; glyphs, scrolling and controls may differ. | Test one primary supported combination first; disclose secondary gaps. |
| Keyboard, reduced motion and readable states | The mountain cannot be the only explanation or block controls. | [WCAG](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) and [status-message guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) provide requirements to check, not a compliance certificate. | Local data expectations | Storage is origin-scoped and generally best effort. | [Storage guidance](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web) and [WebKit policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/) do not support calling IndexedDB permanent. |
| Public build/source and accurate provenance | Judges need accessible current work, with truthful build history. | Verify signed out. A pushed review branch does not prove public visibility or that the default branch contains the prototype. |

Do not add another recovery-engine rewrite because a browser can suspend. Test the existing conservative contract, fix a reproduced failure and describe limits accurately.

## 8. Demo that proves the product

Keep the existing 2:25 draft as a starting point. [Devpost's presentation guidance](https://info.devpost.com/blog/how-to-present-a-successful-hackathon-demo) favours a quick problem setup and a working resolution. Its [video guidance](https://help.devpost.com/article/84-video-making-best-practices) recommends a narrated screencast, early explanation, rehearsal and upload buffer. Those are practical recommendations, not a guarantee of a high score.

Suggested proof sequence:

- First eight seconds: show a useful saved instruction and its work reference. “I left this unfinished. Here is the exact move I saved.” This is a creative target, not a research-established eight-second threshold.
- Briefly establish one realistic goal and what finished means; skip lengthy form entry with an honest edit, not a simulated successful save.
- Show actual work context, Pause, a concrete saved cue and campsite. Clearly label any fictional fixture.
- Reload the real-record flow, show the saved place still present, open the resource, and explicitly Resume. Do not use volatile fictional mode as persistence proof.
- Complete an action, briefly show Undo/recovery if time permits, then distinguish all-checked review from confirmed finish and the summit/history.
- Close with the audience/problem, one honest observation if obtained, and the local-only boundary. Explain actual Skill Pack contribution in the submission text. Do not claim research results as product outcomes.

Most differentiated moment: leaving unfinished work feels safe because the next move and its location are preserved. The mountain should make that transition legible. A summit is supporting proof, not the reason the tool exists.

## 9. Remaining-time decision rule

At 16:48 IST there were roughly 2 hours 12 minutes to the user's 19:00 prototype target, and 1 hour 12 minutes to 18:00. This is the user's working deadline. The event page lists 26 October 17:00 EDT, which converts to **27 October 02:30 IST**; that later deadline is not a reason to spend today's submission buffer on more features.

Use a rolling budget from when this report is read:

1. **20–30 minutes:** independent correction review plus the short real-work observation. If a critical state/storage failure appears, it displaces optional work.
2. **One bounded pass only, 20–35 minutes:** address the most important observed obstacle. Prefer saved-place discovery; use Copy saved place only if discovery works and portability is wanted. Explicit old-note clearing is a reliability candidate, not something to bundle invisibly into polish.
3. **Freeze by approximately 18:00:** no new feature starts after the freeze. Any earlier overrun reduces optional work, not the protected delivery hour.
4. **Protect at least 60 minutes:** user visual decision, public-source/host choices and authorization, final-origin checks, rehearsal, recording and upload preparation. Upload processing can exceed this, so start it early. These tasks require actual execution; this research does not complete them.

If there is not enough time for a pass plus verification plus the protected delivery window, freeze the current accepted build. More available tokens do not create more human review or recording time. Repeating the full suite after every documentation adjustment is less useful than running appropriate checks once against the final changed runtime.

## Recommended decision

Do not pivot or launch a feature sprint. Review End focus block, test a stranger's first return, and make one evidence-led correction. Preserve the mountain. Use a real work reference. Make the defining saved-place interaction easy to discover. Deliver a truthful, accessible, rehearsed demonstration.

If a single extra utility is justified, choose a portable saved-place brief before streaks, sound, AI or new scenery. It is a practical experiment in reducing adoption friction, not a claim of novelty. Trail's weakest criterion will still be innovation until its particular interaction or observed value earns the distinction. That is a real limitation to address through clarity and evidence, not language that calls an ordinary feature revolutionary.

## Research limitations and source policy

First-party product documentation confirms advertised features, not comparative quality. Original research concerns particular populations/tasks; no clinical, productivity, retention or neurological result has been established for Trail. NN/G and company design accounts are guidance with different evidentiary weight from controlled studies. No competitor subscriptions, actual event entries, winning-video review or interviews were conducted. Inaccessible or ambiguous search results were excluded from substantive claims. Sources were checked on 25 October 2026; product capabilities and event pages can change.

No runtime/test edits, commit, push, deployment, submission or external messages were made by this research pass. Only the research plan and this report were added; concurrent implementation changes belong to their own evidence.
