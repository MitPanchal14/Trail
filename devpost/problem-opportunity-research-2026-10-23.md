# Build With AI: painful problems, credible opportunities, and the pivot decision

Research date: **23 October 2026, India time**. Research and recommendations only; this document does not approve a pivot or alter Trail's scope.

## Decision first

**For this deadline, my recommendation is to concentrate Trail around returning to interrupted research, and prove that experience with real users.** Its existing work creates a delivery advantage, but the reason to continue must be a convincing user result, not sunk cost.

**If choosing a new project, my first candidate to validate is ReturnProof:** help someone whose online return was collected but whose refund is unresolved assemble a checked, source-linked account of what happened and what is still missing. **CareRelay** is the strongest alternative when we can immediately reach a family sharing care responsibilities. Neither is an uncontested invention. Both need a narrower, demonstrably better experience than existing products.

I would not claim a percentage chance of winning. The submission field is unknown, the public gallery is unpublished, and no source can turn a pain score into a win probability. This is a comparative product judgment, not a forecast.

The best candidate combines an expensive recurring annoyance, reachable users, usable evidence, one complete workflow, and an outcome a viewer can recognize immediately. A national-scale problem can be a poor hackathon choice when its bottleneck is physical infrastructure, institutional authority, or unavailable data.

## What the competition rewards

The official rules specify four equally weighted criteria: design, potential impact, innovation/idea, and presentation. They require an end-to-end working function, Skill Pack planning documents, public source, and a public demonstration shorter than three minutes. Deadline: **26 October 2026, 5 p.m. EDT / 27 October, 2:30 a.m. IST**. [Official rules](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/rules).

The overview currently omits Potential Impact from its displayed criteria; the rules include it. I use the rules for evaluation. The overview emphasizes a small working application, planned and built with the Skill Pack. It also specifies an empty-folder start during the submission window. [Overview](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/).

The public gallery says it has not been published. Participant counts are not submission counts and cannot reveal the quality or categories of competing projects. [Gallery](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/project-gallery).

My implication: optimize for a complete and credible transformation, with a clear audience and an obvious result. An ambitious platform described in slides is weaker than a small useful product demonstrated working. Runtime AI is a design choice; the stated tool requirement concerns building with the Skill Pack.

## How I treated the evidence

- **Administrative evidence:** actual complaints, amounts, or dispute records. Strong evidence of observed activity; not a representative sample or the total addressable market.
- **Survey and field research:** useful when the population, sample and question are known. A finding about US caregivers does not become a statistic about Indian families.
- **Product documentation:** evidence that a competitor advertises a capability. I did not purchase or test these products, verify their accuracy, or accept their savings claims as independent results.
- **Problem banks and community posts:** useful discovery and interview material. Votes, scores, and anecdotes do not establish prevalence, willingness to pay, or solution effectiveness.
- **Our proposed workflows:** hypotheses. The examples below are invented scenarios, not customer cases or demonstrated implementation.

This research included official hackathon pages, Razorpay's page and interactive categories, government complaint reporting, survey methodology, a caregiving study, and direct competitor checks. It did **not** include customer interviews, private datasets, paid-tool trials, or a fresh runtime audit of Trail. Trail comparison uses the current local scope, PRD and dated handoff, read during this session.

## Razorpay: useful signals and a crucial limitation

Razorpay advertises 10,000+ curated problems from 50,000 people. In the retrieved B2B table, invoice administration scores **67.5**, negotiating payment terms **82.8**, industrial technician delays **78.5**, and outbound pickup accountability **79**. Its prominent themes also include elder care, rental hazards, and used-car uncertainty. I inspected additional Consumer Services and Real Estate categories in the live page. [Fix My Itch](https://razorpay.com/m/fix-my-itch/).

I did not find a sufficiently detailed sampling method, score formula, per-problem respondent count, or willingness-to-pay measure in the material inspected. Treat the scores as the site's ranking, not scientifically calibrated severity.

The important product distinction is **whether the proposed app controls the bottleneck**. Organizing invoice facts can reduce administrative work. It cannot give a small supplier negotiating power over a large buyer. An app can prepare a repair request; it cannot create a technician in a location where none is available. Do not optimize for the largest number while ignoring this distinction.

### 1. ReturnProof — strongest new candidate to validate

**Person and moment:** an Indian online shopper whose item has been picked up, but whose refund status remains unresolved after multiple support exchanges.

**The product promise:** “Know which facts support your case, which facts are missing, and leave with a checked account you can use.” Avoid claiming to recover the money automatically.

**Small end-to-end flow:**

1. Supply an order record, pickup record and support messages as a few images or pasted text.
2. AI proposes dated events and amounts, each linked to its source.
3. The app flags mismatched order IDs, conflicting promises, missing pickup confirmation or uncertain amounts.
4. The person corrects and approves the timeline.
5. Export a concise case summary, evidence index and unresolved questions; preserve the case for a later update.

**Memorable demonstration:** two screenshots disagree about the refund date. The app shows both sources instead of inventing a single answer. A partial credit is entered and confirmed, so the requested remaining amount changes transparently. Reloading retains the unresolved case.

**AI earns its place** by extracting messy evidence and locating contradictions. Arithmetic, state changes and export structure should be deterministic. “Refund initiated” must remain distinct from “credit observed and confirmed.” An absent credit in a limited statement excerpt is not proof that no credit occurred elsewhere.

**Competition is substantial:** NCH already offers multiple entry channels; a new filing interface is not inherently needed. Jagrik advertises complaint documents and gap checking, while Grahak-Nyay explores a consumer-law assistant. Our narrower evidence-reconciliation experience would need to win in an actual comparison. [NCH](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/pages/nch), [Jagrik](https://jagrik.com/consumer-complaint-guide/), [Grahak-Nyay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04854).

**First test:** three people with recent refund disputes, using voluntarily supplied redacted artifacts. Observe reconstruction time and mistakes using their current method, then our prototype with an equivalent case. Check order identity, amount, event dates, missing evidence and unsupported assertions. A small trial supports a specific usability result, not a recovery-rate claim.

**Stop condition:** participants already have clear records and their dominant problem is a merchant refusing to cooperate. In that situation our app mainly produces a prettier document without addressing their bottleneck.

**Build boundary:** one return-refund category, a handful of files, reviewed extraction and export. Court filing, legal strategy, inbox access, automated messages and bank integrations are outside the initial proof of concept. Public demonstration uses fictional records.

### 2. CareRelay — strongest human-impact alternative

**Person and moment:** two siblings or relatives sharing the practical care of a parent; one has to take over after a visit or appointment.

**The product promise:** “See what changed, what still needs doing, and who has acknowledged it.”

**Small flow:** paste a few updates and an appointment notice; review extracted logistics; show changed or conflicting dates, unresolved tasks, source and last-confirmed time; assign an owner; have the next person acknowledge the handoff. Persist the actual shared state.

**Memorable demonstration:** a new notice changes an appointment, but transport is still attached to the old date. The app surfaces the conflict and leaves transport unassigned until a person resolves it. It must not silently treat the latest message as authoritative.

**AI's role:** turn informal updates into reviewed facts and flag possible conflicts. Human confirmation determines the accepted schedule and ownership. A concise handoff is useful only if it reduces the existing work of communicating.

**Competition:** Caring Village advertises care plans, calendar and named task owners. Alongside advertises unresolved items, changes and next tasks in a handoff. ShiftCare advertises AI handover summaries. A summary-plus-checklist clone has a weak novelty argument. [Caring Village](https://caringvillage.com/app/), [Alongside](https://alongsidefamily.com/caregiver-handoff-app), [ShiftCare](https://help.shiftcare.com/en/articles/14817397-using-the-ai-client-summary).

There is also a real institutional signal: the US Administration for Community Living launched a challenge seeking practical caregiver AI. This corroborates interest in the problem, not endorsement of our solution or eligibility for that separate contest. [ACL](https://acl.gov/caregiver-ai-competition).

**First test:** one caregiver pair, two independent logistics-only handoffs. Can the receiving person identify the current appointment, unresolved task, owner and uncertainty without calling the outgoing person? Measure preparation and reading time, missed information and corrections. Do not claim reduced hospitalizations or safer medical care.

**Stop condition:** the pair will not maintain another app, the handoff takes longer than their messages, or a summary obscures information they consider critical.

**Build boundary:** appointments, transport, documents and practical responsibilities. Clinical decisions, diagnosis, medication changes and emergency monitoring would create a different project. Shared access and acknowledgement must actually work; switching fictional personas locally is only a labeled simulation.

### 3. DepositProof — strongest visual evidence demo

**Person and moment:** a renter documenting the condition of a room when moving in, before disagreements about an existing defect arise.

**The product promise:** “Leave with a useful condition record and know what you forgot to capture.”

**Small flow:** follow a short capture checklist; review AI descriptions tied to original photos; identify missing coverage; record both parties' comments if available; export the original images and condition notes. Later, compare a matching move-out view and mark disputed or inconclusive differences.

**Memorable demonstration:** the app finds a pre-existing mark in a move-in image relevant to a proposed deduction. It also says “cannot compare reliably” when views or lighting differ too much. The negative case is part of the useful behavior.

**Competition:** InventoryBase already supports check-in/check-out comparison and photo-generated report content; RentCheck advertises AI Damage Assist. We cannot present photo comparison or AI captions as a new category. [InventoryBase comparison](https://support.inventorybase.com/en/articles/3491276-conducting-check-out-inspections), [BaseAI](https://support.inventorybase.com/en/articles/11163891-baseai-smarter-reports-with-inventory-base), [RentCheck](https://help.getrentcheck.com/en/articles/13521645-ai-damage-assist).

**First test:** three renters document a room using their normal process and a guided process. Have a separate reviewer identify which evidence supports a specific condition assertion. Count missing views and unsupported conclusions; do not claim legal admissibility or guaranteed deposit recovery.

**Stop condition:** the landlord's refusal to acknowledge the record is the primary problem, or the only differentiator is lower price.

**Build boundary:** one room, visible surface condition, original files and reviewed observations. Images cannot certify absence of mold, concealed defects, structural risk or pests. A timestamp or file hash alone cannot prove where, when or under what circumstances a photo was taken.

### 4. DueProof — severe pain, but a close competitor lowers its rank

**Person and moment:** a freelancer faces “the work was not accepted” or “that payment covered a different milestone,” with facts scattered across a contract, messages and payment records.

**Small flow:** supply those records; review a source-linked scope/acceptance timeline; confirm partial payments; separate agreed and disputed amounts; export a factual packet and an editable follow-up draft.

**Memorable demonstration:** a supposed approval is revealed to refer to an earlier version. The app exposes the evidence gap instead of selecting the quote most favorable to the freelancer.

**The difficult competitive finding:** Accordio's Payment Dispute Helper explicitly advertises reconstructing agreed scope, deliverables and written approvals from contracts and messages. Chaser advertises dispute and broken-promise flags. This significantly weakens the originality argument. [Accordio](https://accordio.ai/ai-agents/payment-dispute-helper), [Chaser](https://help.chaserhq.com/debtor-relationship-management-dashboard).

Do not build an invoice-reminder app as the supposed gap: Zoho already documents manual and automated reminders. [Zoho](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/zoho-invoice/power-dialer/articles/how-do-i-send-reminders-for-overdue-invoices).

**First test:** two freelancers reconstruct a closed dispute with known facts. Check whether the packet saves effort and correctly preserves ambiguity. No forecast of debt recovery; organizing proof does not force a solvent or insolvent buyer to pay.

**Stop condition:** no usable artifact access, or participants see no material advantage over their current tools. Choose this only if a specific local-language or artifact-handling need is demonstrated, not assumed.

### 5. FormBridge — valuable when anchored to one actual barrier

**Person and moment:** someone who cannot independently complete a particular public PDF or poorly labeled form with their keyboard or screen reader.

**Small flow:** support one selected, legitimately usable blank form; present its questions and instructions in an accessible sequence; let the user review answers and missing fields; produce a checked mapping or filled output while retaining the source instructions.

**Memorable demonstration:** a target user completes a previously blocked section without another person reading and transcribing it. That is much stronger than showing an automated accessibility score.

**Competition and evidence limits:** Adobe already documents accessible PDF form creation and has a document AI assistant; Deque provides automated and AI-assisted testing. A generic scanner or “chat with PDF” is not the opportunity. WebAIM's web-input results support investigating barriers but do not quantify the selected PDF's problem. [Adobe accessible PDFs](https://helpx.adobe.com/in/acrobat/using/creating-accessible-pdfs.html), [Adobe AI](https://helpx.adobe.com/acrobat/using/get-ai-generated-answers.html), [Deque](https://www.deque.com/axe/devtools/extension/).

**First test:** one target user, the exact form, an actual screen reader/keyboard workflow and a checked export. AI can explain instructions and propose mappings; it must not invent eligibility rules or answers.

**Stop condition:** no target user can participate, output cannot be checked faithfully, or the original form already works with an existing tool. Do not claim universal PDF support or accessibility certification.

## Trail: candid assessment against the alternatives

Our local scope already identifies the important problem: starting unfamiliar work and reconstructing context after a gap. The current PRD contains explicit resume, preserved stopping context, reviewed AI actions and progress linked to completion. The October 21 handoff records implementation and test evidence, but also pending hands-on acceptance and limited live AI-quality validation. Those are dated local records, not fresh results from this research session.

The broad pitch “AI breaks tasks down and rewards productivity with a mountain” is weakly differentiated. Goblin Tools advertises task breakdown and a one-task-at-a-time timer workflow. Tiimo advertises spoken/typed planning and breakdown. [Magic ToDo](https://android.goblin.tools/ToDo), [Taskmaster](https://goblin.tools/Taskmaster), [Tiimo](https://www.tiimoapp.com/).

The stronger product hypothesis is:

> Return to difficult research with the exact context you left and one action you can actually execute.

The landscape can give that experience identity and a satisfying memory of work. It cannot substitute for reducing the work needed to restart. Treat the combination as a differentiation hypothesis, not a novel scientific intervention.

**A focused demonstration:** a fictional researcher is comparing two competing explanations. They record the unresolved question and the next check, close the app, then return later. Trail restores their supplied context and the accepted action. They explicitly resume, perform a small real step and record completion. Only completion adds an earned checkpoint. The mountain makes the record recognizable; the restored context provides the utility.

**The decisive validation:** recruit three people who do interruption-heavy research or project work. Observe a real pause and later return. Record capture time, whether the context is adequate, whether the next instruction is executable, and whether the person actually starts the work. Report individuals and failure cases. A few sessions cannot establish improved productivity or retention.

If the task requires “read the entire paper” or “finish the analysis,” the first action may remain too broad. If saving context feels like another administrative chore, the mechanism can fail even when persistence works perfectly. These matter more than another screen or animation.

**Why I favor Trail for this entry:** there is an existing end-to-end foundation and a reachable first user. A pivot incurs extraction, review, storage, export, deployment and domain-validation work before it can be judged fairly. But if a real return attempt remains confusing and cannot be repaired within the remaining time, we should not protect Trail merely because it exists.

## An evidence gate before any pivot

Spend a short, bounded session on **Trail versus ReturnProof**, not a new week collecting ideas. The following thresholds are proposed decision rules, not statistical standards or work already completed:

1. Reach three relevant people for each candidate where possible. Ask about the last actual incident and current workaround, not whether they like our pitch.
2. For ReturnProof, establish that at least two people actually struggled to reconstruct evidence and can demonstrate that with safe, redacted material. For Trail, establish that people lose an actionable stopping point after a real interruption.
3. Compare with a plain note/template and the closest existing product. Identify the exact step our version handles better. Convenience alone can matter, but must be observed.
4. Check we can build and validate the complete workflow in the available time. An extraction success without correction, persistence and usable output is incomplete.
5. Choose one. Reserve the final substantial block for usability fixes, public-access checks and the demonstration.

Suggested schedule: **23 October** decision and first user evidence; **24 October** one complete usable journey; **25 October** observe trials and fix failures; **26 October** stabilize the submission and record the video well before the overnight IST deadline. If starting late, reduce scope rather than borrowing from submission time.

For the demonstration, show the trigger, messy input, reviewed transformation, result, saved state and one meaningful failure case. Use a source statistic briefly, then let the working product carry the argument. Report measured small-sample results honestly. Do not label generated documents “money recovered,” an acknowledged handoff “care improved,” or a completed timer “productivity improved.”

## What remains uncertain

- Which specific workflow is most painful for the people we can reach now.
- Whether evidence preparation, cooperation, or institutional delay is the dominant refund/payment bottleneck.
- Whether any proposed difference is valuable enough to motivate switching from an existing tool.
- Whether caregivers would maintain another shared record and who would do that work.
- Whether Trail reduces restart friction sufficiently to justify its recording step.
- The competing submission field and the judges' eventual assessments.

**Recommendation:** keep Trail as the submission baseline; test its return experience immediately. Validate ReturnProof as the first fresh alternative, and choose CareRelay only with an available caregiver pair. Change direction only when firsthand evidence and a complete delivery path outweigh the advantage of the existing build. There is no evidence here that supports a guaranteed win or an untouched market.
