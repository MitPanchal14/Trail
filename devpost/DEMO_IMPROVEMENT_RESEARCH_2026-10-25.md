# Trail — research for the submission demo

25 October 2026, research follow-up requested by the user while away from their workstation. This is a source-backed product/demo review, not a user study or a prediction of judging results. Runtime and accepted terrain/layout are unchanged. The earlier 21-file completion bundle remains a snapshot of the implementation pass; revised materials in this follow-up supersede its recording drafts.

## Recommendation

**Make the return to unfinished work the main demonstration. Show the saved next move in the opening eight seconds, then prove save → leave → reopen → Open work → explicit Resume before spending time on the summit.** The mountain should make the loop memorable, while the saved instruction makes its practical value clear.

Trail already has a working loop and reliability evidence. The largest remaining risks are whether judges immediately understand its purpose, whether they can access the current build/source, and whether the actual Skill Pack usage is explained truthfully. Adding streaks, audio, AI or another mountain style would leave those risks unresolved.

This conclusion is my synthesis of official criteria, organizer guidance, a small first-party product comparison and the current implementation/drafts. It is not a measured improvement or a claim that Trail is unique in the market.

## What the sources establish

### The event rewards a coherent, demonstrated product

The official criteria give equal weight to design, potential impact, innovation/idea and presentation. The initial gate concerns the Skill Pack and planning artifacts. The working project must match its demonstration. There is no requirement in the reviewed rules to add an AI model feature inside the app. Adding one solely because the event says “AI” would be unnecessary scope. [Official rules](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/rules)

The organizer's written recap of the five-project “Will It Ship” review emphasizes clear purpose/audience early, quick judge access, distinctive design, realistic demo content, plain wording and showing the app promptly. I read this recap; **I did not watch/transcribe the linked recording**, so no other spoken feedback is attributed to it. [Organizer recap](https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/updates/47209-will-it-ship-recording-is-up)

Devpost's general demo guidance recommends a brief problem setup, a working demonstration that resolves that problem, then a short close. It advises avoiding routine setup consuming the allotted time. [Devpost presentation guidance](https://info.devpost.com/blog/how-to-present-a-successful-hackathon-demo)

**Application to Trail:** a long goal-entry/timer introduction makes it resemble a familiar task timer. A saved-place opening gives the later interruption/reopen sequence a clear question to answer. The existing script already shows the app early, but its full return payoff starts around 1:05; the revised script moves the return sequence to 0:54 and uses a real saved-place preview at 0:00. These are editorial targets, not attention-span research findings.

### A return plan has a plausible research basis, with important limits

Leroy and Glomb's 2018 paper reports four studies of attention residue under anticipated time pressure when interrupted. Its ready-to-resume intervention involved briefly reflecting on/planning a return. The reported outcomes concern attention and performance on the **interrupting task**; this is not a trial of Trail or proof that Trail reduces time to restart work. [University research record and abstract](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/tasks-interrupted-how-anticipating-time-pressure-on-resumption-of/)

A University of Washington article describes this work and the return-planning intervention in accessible language. It is useful background, not a second independent validation study of this app. [University explanation](https://www.uwb.edu/news/2024/05/17/an-ongoing-study-on-the-success-of-staying-focused)

**Application to Trail:** ask for a small concrete next move and necessary context. Keep it optional and cheap to write; do not turn leaving into another planning project. The current short next-move field and collapsed optional extras fit that design hypothesis. We still need to observe whether people actually use and understand them. No percentage/productivity/clinical claim belongs in the pitch.

## Revised demo story

The broad product audience stays intact. The recording uses **one** concrete example: preparing a short planning update for volunteers organizing a community workshop. All content is staged and labelled.

- Goal: **Prepare the community workshop update**.
- Finished means: **A short update explains what is ready, what is blocked and who needs to do what next.**
- Actions: **Choose the three points volunteers need to know**; **Draft the update with clear next steps**; **Check names, facts and next-step owners**.
- Saved next move: **Under Next steps, add who will confirm the room booking. Then reread the opening.**
- Where stopped: **Activities and volunteer roles are drafted. The room is still unconfirmed.**

Keep the staged scratch draft visible if demonstrating actual work. If a public owned draft is unavailable, retain `example.com` with an on-screen placeholder label; say Open work opens a saved reference, never that it reopened a draft it did not contain. Do not create/publish an external document without authorization.

The revised shot sequence is: saved-place preview → brief creation → first completion/movement → interrupted draft/campsite → actual close/reopen → reference/explicit Resume → completion/review/confirmed summit → kept work. The saved-place preview must be captured from the same staged task; it may be placed earlier in the edit with an honest label. It is not a fabricated first-use state.

## Human observation protocol for when the user returns

This is a proposal, not a completed research study. Prefer a real piece of work and a natural interruption over an artificial speed test.

1. Let the user work briefly, then leave a next move without coaching the exact wording.
2. After returning, ask: “What would you do next?” Observe what they look at/click before explaining the UI.
3. Note whether they can find the reference and understand that Open work does not start focus.
4. Ask what they think the campsite means and whether all-checked means the goal is already finished.
5. Record actual wording/confusion and a concrete observation. A sample of one is exploratory; do not turn it into a percentage or general productivity claim.

Possible small follow-up fixes, **only if reproduced**: clearer campsite/review caption, a confusing button label, an obscured control. Preserve accepted layout and timing/storage contracts. No research-derived demand for a new feature has been established.

## Screenshot and recording choices

Use a readable saved next move as the main gallery image; a tiny complete-dashboard shot can bury the point. The general Devpost guide recommends a 3:2 thumbnail and relevant gallery images; verify the live event form's exact limits. [Submission guide](https://help.devpost.com/article/126-know-your-submission-steps)

Suggested gallery: (1) saved move+reference+campsite, (2) one completed checkpoint with traveller underway, (3) explicit review/finish distinction, (4) retained finished goal with notes. Caption each staged fixture honestly. Keep the mountain in context with the action list rather than implying its height measures performance. No stock art pack or new generated terrain is necessary.

During video editing, hold the saved instruction long enough to read, keep the cursor out of its text, and preserve a normal-speed completion transition. Use visible shortened-work/typing cuts; do not accelerate the focus clock. Mention conservative storage/recovery briefly after the product proof. Put detailed test counts in the written evidence rather than reading a checklist aloud.

## Questions the recording should answer

| Likely question | Answer the existing build can demonstrate |
| --- | --- |
| Why use this when I already have task notes? | The saved next move is the return screen's main instruction, paired with context/reference and a deliberate Resume choice. Show the sequence; do not claim competitors cannot do it. |
| Does leaving destroy my progress? | Saved work remains in this browser; history/effort are retained. Clearing site data can lose it, and uncertain away time is not credited. No sync/backup guarantee. |
| Does Open work start the timer or inspect my document? | No. It opens the user-supplied reference in another tab; Start/Resume is separate. |
| What does the mountain measure? | Checked action states; route geometry is illustrative. Recorded effort is separate, not attention or distance. |
| Why does all-checked still open review? | The person decides whether their definition of finished is met; they can add work, keep open or confirm. |
| What did the Skill Pack change about the build? | The user must supply the actual steps/decisions. Installed metadata and present planning docs alone do not answer this truthfully. |
